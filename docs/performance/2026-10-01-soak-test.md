# The live demo over twenty game days — soak test, 2026-10-01

Brendan approved this test on 2026-10-01 (feature #14) before the Windows playtest build. The question: does the
nine-resident village leak memory, objects or nodes, or slow down, over a long session, and does Restart demo leave
anything behind? The harness, the report tool and their choices are in decision
[0921](../decisions/0921-the-demo-soak-test-and-its-restart-leak-watch.md). Two leaks were fixed in this change
(decisions [0922](../decisions/0922-the-kitchen-s-rounds-to-come-back-to-hold-it-weakly.md) and
[0923](../decisions/0923-farm-alert-keys-are-let-go-after-their-day.md)).

## Verdict

- **No growth and no drift over twenty game days at nine residents.** The report raised no flag.
  - Static memory's daily floor rose 0.37 MB from day 1 to day 19, and moved 30 KB from day 12 on.
  - The object count rose 48 in the same days, and has stood at 13 612–13 624 since day 6.
  - Nodes rose by 10. Resources and orphan nodes did not move.
  - Frame time did not drift: the median frame was 1.3–1.8 ms on every day.
  - Not one error or warning was printed.
  - Freeing the village at the end left nothing of it alive and unreachable.
- **Restart demo leaked the whole cast. It is fixed, and now leaves nothing behind.**
  - **Before the fix:** once the cook or a water-drawer had been called away mid-round, a Restart left the old village
    alive for good: its brains, kitchen, pantry, cast space, routing desk, tunnel network and feeds. At 25 residents
    that was 110 objects. The two Restarts tried at nine residents before the fix left nothing: neither came after an
    interrupted round, and the cycle forms only then.
  - **The cycle:** brain → unfinished job → kitchen → brain (decision 0922).
  - **After the fix:** 37 Restarts at nine residents left 0 objects unreachable, and so did one at 25.
- **Two small leftovers, neither a leak that matters for the playtest.**
  - Each Restart leaves 2 objects held by something still alive. The owner was not found; see "Open".
  - The farm's said-once alert keys grew a few a day. That is fixed (decision 0923).
- **The engine's "resources still in use at exit" lines were the ambient sounds**, not a leak. When the process
  quits, the streams still playing are counted. The runner now parses them apart (decision 0921 §7).

## Read this first: what these numbers are

- **This measures the demo, not the qualification floor.**
  - It ran headless, in the editor binary (debug GDScript), on a shared development Mac (Apple M5 Pro, 18 cores).
  - The demo holds about 1.07 GB of staged assets from the start.
  - "Static memory" is the engine's own count (`Performance.MEMORY_STATIC`), so a growth of 0.4 MB shows against it.
  - It is not REQ-SET-163's simulation-owned budget.
- **4x, fixed 60 Hz step.** Each frame hands the village 1/60 s of demo time, and the engine runs uncapped. A game day
  is therefore exactly 9 000 frames whatever the load. "Frame work" is a frame's real duration less the harness's own
  time (decision 0561 §4).
- **The machine was shared, but quiet for the committed run:** a 1-minute load of 9–11 on 18 cores.
  - The report samples the load and the Godot process's CPU time every 5 s, so it can tell a busier machine from a
    slower game.
  - Contention moves the per-frame CPU a little too, so day-to-day changes of ±20 % in both are load, not the game.
- **What churns.**
  - Everything that runs on its own:
    - meals at 07:00 and 17:00 (171 batches cooked, 291 portions eaten);
    - the night at dusk and dawn, and songs;
    - the work board's routine tasks, the woods and the farm;
    - threats, about one a game day. There were 19, each a critical pause lifted the next frame;
    - notices (320 posted, the feed full at 128 from day 9) and incidents (21).
  - What the harness adds, as a player would:
    - the pantry topped up at 04:00 (otherwise the village stops cooking by day 9: decision 0911);
    - a work party of eight ordered to the square at 10:00 and let go at 11:00;
    - in the restart runs, Restart demo.
- **Floors.** The growth test reads each game day's smallest hourly sample: what the day kept, not its busiest hour.

Reproduce:

```
python3 tools/soak_test.py --out-dir <dir> --days 20                                   # the committed run, ~5-8 min
python3 tools/soak_test.py --out-dir <dir> --tag restarts --days 3 --restart-every-hours 6
python3 tools/soak_report.py <dir>/soak.json --md <out.md> --json <out.json>          # re-judge a run
```

## Restart demo

### The leak, before the fix

A Restart after the first supper at 25 residents (run S) left **110 objects of the old village alive and unreachable**:

- the brains, with their RNGs (25 + 25);
- the cast's space, nav, nav graphs (4) and grids (5), and the routing desk;
- the kitchen with its meal store, takes, nourishment, places and two tasks, and the farm pantry and its storage;
- the tunnel network, router, queue and ground, the rooms and fixtures, and the spoil hauls;
- the water map, links, bridges and crossings, and the swim state and motion;
- the notices, the incidents, the news clock and the calendar.

The engine's exit report said the same: "180 ObjectDB instances leaked", "41 resources still in use". This is the
cycle the scale test suspected (decision 0561, "Also found"). Every Restart after a cook's round had been interrupted
kept another whole cast's logic.

**The owner and the fix.** `kitchen.gd unfinished_of` built the cook's and the drawer's rounds-to-come-back-to from
the kitchen's own methods. `unfinished_job.gd` keeps its take-back's object alive on purpose, the brain keeps the job,
and the kitchen keeps every brain. The round is now a small object holding the kitchen's method Callable, which does
not keep the kitchen alive (decision 0922). Owners: the kitchen (decision 0381) and the resume list (decision 0205).

### After the fix

| Run | Restarts | Unreachable | Held (each restart) | Objects right after each restart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E, every 6 game hours (final code) | 11 | 0 | 45 | 13 553 → 13 575 |
| R1, every game day | 5 | 0 | 45 | 13 555 → 13 565 |
| R2, every 6 game hours | 11 | 0 | 45 | 13 553 → 13 575 |
| R3, every 2 game hours, `--verbose` | 10 | 0 | 45 | 13 553 → 13 573 |
| S′, 25 residents, after supper | 1 | 0 | 45 | — |

- **What is "held" is shared state, not a leak.** The 45 are the same after every restart:
  - the settlement simulation's owners, which GameManager keeps: entity directory, farming, needs, jobs, the
    schedule, the command queue and its dispatch, the weather, the clock and the rest;
  - forest_root_field.gd's two baked root fields, a static cache by design (decision 0301);
  - the water map, which a script's static member holds.
- **A Restart takes about 4 s headless** (13 frames, mostly the boot's prewarm). It is not measured.

## Errors

Runs D and E heard no error and no warning. Their logs hold no script or engine error, and no exit-time report beyond
sounds.

- **The exit reports, explained.** Earlier runs printed "N resources still in use at exit" now and then: 3, 13 or 21.
- **What `--verbose` named:** `amb_stream_01.ogg` and `amb_wind_01.wav`, their packet sequence and their playbacks.
  These are the ambient sounds still playing as the process quit. The audio server lets go of them on a mix that a
  headless run never reaches, even after the village is freed.
- **What the runner does now:** it runs Godot with `--verbose` and counts those audio lines apart. Any other line of
  the exit report is still flagged as a leak.

## Open

- **+2 objects per Restart, held by something alive.** After each Restart the object count is exactly 2 higher than
  after the one before, in runs E, R1, R2 and R3 alike.
  - The `--verbose` exit report is empty, so whatever holds them is freed at exit: an autoload, the root, or the
    engine.
  - The watch cannot see them, because they are not reachable from the old village's script members.
  - The owner was not found. A player restarting a hundred times keeps 200 small objects.
- **What a captured lambda variable holds is invisible to the watch** (a GDScript limit). An object reachable only
  that way is neither watched nor counted as reachable (decision 0921).

## Recommendations

1. **Ship the playtest build on this.** Nothing grows or slows in twenty game days, and Restart demo no longer leaks.
2. **Run the soak before each playtest build.**
   - `python3 tools/soak_test.py --out-dir <dir> --days 20`, about 5–8 minutes.
   - A restart run: `--days 3 --restart-every-hours 6`.
   - The suite's 5-hour run already catches a Restart that leaves objects unreachable.
   - When the kitchen, the cast or the night change, also run the one-day slow test (`REDWALL_SLOW_TESTS=1`).
3. **Run a windowed soak on the Windows build's machine class.** Headless shows no video memory and no render cost.
   The harness samples `video_kb` and `texture_kb` already. A windowed run needs two things:
   - the scale test's windowed set-up (vsync off, the window's size: `scale_test.gd _initialize`);
   - a `--windowed` switch in `tools/soak_test.py`.

   Not done here. Estimate: half a day.
4. **A 25-resident 20-day soak.** At 25 residents the routing cost dominates (the scale test). It was run here only for
   one Restart.
5. **Find the 2 objects per Restart.** Two ways:
   - bisect by building the village without one subsystem at a time;
   - log `Performance.OBJECT_COUNT` around each autoload's re-registration (UIManager's `register_hud`, the
     settlement's boot).
6. **A light-touch soak.** Decision 0911's scripted player, instead of this harness's top-up, would also churn sowing,
   fishing and the Work screen's orders. This is a proposal for Brendan (decision 0921, Consequences).
7. **Remove the scale test's tolerated exit line** (`test_scale_stress.gd EXIT_LEAK`) once a run of that test no
   longer prints it. Its sounds may still print one; that test's owner should parse them as `tools/soak_test.py` does.
