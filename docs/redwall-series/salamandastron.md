## Central reading

The title's fortress is only one center of vulnerability. In the inspected Abbey passages, illness reorganizes work, exposes limits of expertise and exhausts the people responsible for everyone else. Alongside this, Urthstripe's household shows that protective authority can fail to communicate affection. The stronghold and the home are both places where strength alone does not answer the problem. [SA-P01; SA-P03–05]

Urthstripe's concern for Mara is genuine, but their relationship has deteriorated into avoidance, severity and rebellion. He feels the gap between military command and parenthood. Even his exchange with Sapwood combines physical power with concern for the person he accidentally hurts. This supports an experienced keeper who can be capable without being emotionally omniscient. It does not approve a particular failure, family history or relationship for Rowan. [SA-P01]

The Abbey's illness scenes give the clearest inspected account of care as a network of tasks. Laundry, cooled water, bedding, cleaning, herb gathering, preparing medicine and attending patients require different hands and knowledge. When Hollyberry becomes incapacitated, having ingredients is insufficient: another resident does not know how to blend them. That is a specific distinction between material availability and retained expertise. A simulation can learn from it, but this research cannot substitute for an exact care-scheduling and fallback specification. [SA-P03–05]

## Illness does not measure moral worth

Faith asks what the Abbey did wrong to deserve the fever. This is a grieving character's question, not evidence that illness is punishment. Burrley's absence is understood through his friendship and shared cellar work; the loss interrupts a specific relationship. Tudd's wish that he had died instead conveys grief that cannot be responsibly reduced to a generic productivity deduction. A new scenario should make the missing person legible through established routines, while avoiding a clinical diagnosis or a universal sequence of mourning. [SA-P05]

Care also exposes uneven authority. Vale insists on evidence when residents accuse outsiders, but the same community can dismiss an elder's usefulness or overextend its helpers. The novel contains warmth and hierarchy together. It would be misleading to extract only its best pronouncements and call them a complete, consistently practiced civic code. The game can choose stronger procedural protections under DEC-005 without claiming that every source institution already has them. [SA-P02–05]

The quest for Flowers of Icetor uses inherited knowledge that some characters initially ridicule. That supports the narrative importance of listening across expertise and generations. It does not establish that all folklore is true, that intuition identifies unknown plants safely or that every illness should have a single rare-item cure. The scene's improvised confidence belongs to an adventure narrative. The game's health systems require their own explicit, fictional mechanics. [SA-P03]

## Different homes and different futures

The island refuge in Loambudd's account is found after displacement and travel. It represents a meaningful home outside the Abbey and the mountain. The later succession at Salamandastron expresses family, martial inheritance and material repair through Urthstripe's restored armor. Samkim's account of Redwall, by contrast, locates belonging in autumn air, baking, orchards, stories and familiar company. These are different social and sensory centers. Their architectural variety should not be reduced to skins placed over an identical biography. [SA-P06; SA-P08]

The ending's frame matters as much as the homecoming. Dumble has become the storyteller, Arula a grandmother, and remembered relationships still shape where an elder sleeps and waits. This gives the world's past an everyday presence. It also warns against converting every closing assurance into literal permanent safety: the book has already shown how vulnerable that home can be. [SA-P09]

Thrugg's commemorative bowl and spoon are especially useful material references. Gratitude becomes an object that belongs to a daily practice, rather than only a statue or weapon. It is suitable inspiration for original community props with named makers and recipients. No economic reward or infinite food entitlement should be inferred from the gift. [SA-P07]
