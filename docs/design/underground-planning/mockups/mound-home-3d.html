A mound home: heap, dig, frame, top

The hill is heaped just big enough for the rooms you painted, then the rooms are dug into it, framed with timber ribs, and topped with turf. Doors and windows are items you place on the room's outer cell edges. The hill opens only where you put them. Sizes are illustrative.

1Heap

The heap comes from the rooms you paint: a metre of cover over them, rolling straight down from their outer walls, so every room sits at the hill’s edge. Add a room and the next heap grows the hill.

2Dig

Rooms are dug into the heap like any dug room. A small yard is dug in front of each door you placed, for a plastered front under a timber eave. Every room sits at the hill’s edge, so a window on the turf slope opens straight into its room.

3Frame

The brace phase every dug cell already pays for is drawn as timber arch ribs and arched door frames.

4Top

Thin spots over the rooms are topped up to the cover rule, then turf, shrubs, a garden, the lantern and lit windows. A hearth below sends up a chimney.
