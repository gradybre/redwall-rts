## Central reading

The selected passages frame freedom as an achievement of households, organizers, travelers, engineers and caregivers. Martin is crucial, but the resistance has a history before his arrival and continues making decisions while he recovers. The eventual Abbey is a construction project arising from that social world. It is not simply a reward building placed after a champion wins. [MF-P01–04; MF-P08–09]

Bella's framing story links the harvest and a child's ordinary pleasures to freedom won earlier. The winter opening then shows the inverse: household security eroded by extraction, surveillance and the inability to remain safely at home. Gonff's account describes Verdauga taking half the harvest and the later regime demanding more while woodlanders disappear or leave. This is a political economy of coercion, not a neutral resource tax whose optimal percentage should be copied into a game. The fraction belongs to the novel's account; no harvest yield, levy rate or morale equation is inherited from it. [MF-P01–02]

The resistance council turns scattered capacities into coordinated action. Representation, testimony, disagreements and a deputy's vote precede a rescue decision. Lady Amber questions Gonff's worth; Skipper answers with his previous assistance to hungry families. Trust therefore has a social history. The council is neither a modern universal democracy nor a solitary hero's command interface. Its useful design contribution is that reasons, relationships and practical competence are visible before a collective decision is made. [MF-P03]

## Architecture as a social practice

Brockhall's root-vaulted rooms, hearth, storage and kitchen matter because they shelter a community and receive displaced visitors. They offer concrete support for the user's interest in earth-built homes. The appropriate translation is a room-and-route brief explaining who arrives, who rests, where provisions move and how care reaches residents. A root canopy alone does not establish excavation physics, vertical navigation, ventilation, slope stability or the approved footprint of a multi-level settlement. Those need the separate construction contract. [MF-P04–05]

The flooding plan uses a map, local topography, old watercourses and divided expertise. Dinny, Foremole, Columbine, Skipper and Amber contribute different parts of understanding and execution. Bella emphasizes avoiding the losses of open battle. The landscape becomes a strategic medium, but its transformation is also a choice about whose lives to risk. For future engineering content, show the information and tradeoffs that make a plan intelligible. Do not import a spectacular novel outcome as proof that Godot terrain modification or structural simulation has been designed. [MF-P06]

The conclusion is particularly valuable for the colony layer. Martin's illness gives others room to plan, work and form relationships. Caregivers need relief, visitors are redirected into useful supporting tasks and the future Abbey depends on transport and construction arrangements. An inhabited settlement should continue to possess purpose when its most famous resident is unavailable. This is a narrative principle compatible with ECS; it does not require a bespoke simulation object for each biography. [MF-P08–09]

## Authority, mercy and wonder

Gingivere's reception is a meaningful counterexample to reducing wildcats to a single moral identity. His departure also matters: he can receive gratitude and safety without joining the host community permanently. The same ending treats other defeated creatures much more harshly. Feeding prisoners is followed by forced washing, banishment and a deadline backed by lethal threat. Bella stops further pursuit, but that intervention should not be inflated into a modern prisoner-rights regime. [MF-P06; MF-P08]

Boar's sword lesson says the wielder's integrity matters more than the weapon. The later presentation nevertheless gives the blade extraordinary material capability and a powerful aura of destiny. These ideas coexist. Its production includes an inherited hilt, recovered celestial metal and work performed overnight by named hands; it is not merely an unexplained loot drop. The mysterious carvings deserve the same care: Boar reports that he did not make them, and the scene gives them emotional and prophetic significance. Our uncertain-wonder policy is a deliberate adaptation, not a claim that this source debunks its own prophecy. [MF-P07]
