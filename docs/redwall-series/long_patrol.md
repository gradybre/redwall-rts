## Central reading

The selected sequence moves from Tammo's fantasy of effortless victory to the experience of fear, killing, loss and lasting injury. Yet the novel also retains military pride, formidable comrades and ceremonial honor. Its productive tension is not a simple rejection of soldiering. It distinguishes imagined martial identity from the obligations and costs of protecting a community. In parallel, the Abbey's failing south wall makes ordinary maintenance part of that protection. [LP-P01–03; LP-P07–09]

Tammo's family establishes that vocation is contested even within hare culture. His older siblings prefer farming, while his father admires the military qualities he refuses to let Tammo exercise yet. Mem's intervention arises partly from remembering an earlier family estrangement. Recruitment is therefore embedded in family memory, authority and fear of separation. The text supplies variation that a species-wide “all hares want war” personality rule would erase. It does not supply exact adult ages or a child recruitment system. [LP-P01]

Russa's deathbed advice is unusually important: Tammo need not become a Patroller simply because he can fight. She distinguishes upbringing, skill and chosen life. Earlier, he refuses her suggestion of killing defeated ferrets; later, another comrade checks his desire for vengeance because it would endanger their mission. These scenes make moral judgment relational and unfinished. A tactical success is not the same thing as a mature decision. [LP-P02; LP-P04–05]

## Maintenance, information and the inhabited past

The wall episode is a compact study of leadership failure and repair. Specialists first withhold information from Tansy on the grounds that she has enough responsibilities. She insists on being informed. The inspection uses observation, probing and a plumb line; the later assembly reveals that reassuring language alone does not settle legitimate fear. Viola, previously treated as an irritating fussbudget, proves capable in the crisis. Tansy then explicitly revises her friends' judgment of her. The lesson is to let competence appear through action and to allow residents to be more than their comic introduction. [LP-P03]

The historical journal makes the ground under Redwall part of its remembered past. Construction decisions, a drying lake and buried Kotir leave consequences that emerge much later. This is a strong reference for restoration scenarios: a building's condition can express prior choices rather than random decay. The text does not quantify subsidence or establish a universal geological model. An implementation plan must distinguish authored ruin history from procedural structural failure. [LP-P06]

Food continues throughout investigation and debate. The kitchen's late meal makes scholarship sociable and physically situated. This is more specific than putting a lore book on a decorative shelf: residents read, disagree and solve a present problem while others prepare and share food. A scene brief can preserve those relationships through animation and dialogue without introducing a hidden social-stat subsystem. [LP-P03; LP-P06]

## The aftermath must remain an aftermath

Tammo's distress after the battle directly breaks the opening fantasy. The older Major offers reassurance, while Pasque directs attention to dawn. Neither gesture changes the fact that friends died. The later record explicitly preserves recovery over time, Tammo's limp, Cregga's blindness, a grave site, a poem and an altered weapon serving as a memorial. Rebuilding and celebration happen alongside those losses. “Victory restores everyone” would contradict the inspected ending. [LP-P07–08]

Cregga's movement into care and Russano's upbringing provide a further complication. The text associates the end of her Bloodwrath with blindness and gives her renewed purpose through nurturing a child. Retain her continuing agency and relationships; do not turn disability into a universal moral cure or prescribe caregiving as every injured woman's proper vocation. *Marlfox* and *Taggerung* extend her authority beyond this ending, showing why a single transition should not become her whole character model. [LP-P08–09; MX-P07–08; TG-P05–06]

The final roles also vary within a community: a former chief becomes a cellarkeeper; other groups help repair the wall and then consider leaving. Long-term belonging need not require everyone to settle permanently or retain their wartime rank. [LP-P08]

## Counterchecks and unfinished reading

The ending celebrates military honor and contains revenge and no-quarter language. It is not uniformly anti-war. The study has not continuously audited Cregga's entire pursuit, every patrol encounter or the complete battle's tactics. Those remain necessary before a faithful battle scenario. The source's hundreds of fighters are literary quantities, not permission to exceed the current living-resident cap.
