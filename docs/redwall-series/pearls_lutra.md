## Central reading

The inspected passages move value away from possession alone. Ublaz's power rests partly on controlling a scarce practical resource, while the pearls concentrate desire and loss into portable objects. At the ending, Tansy's decision to discard the treasure is treated as evidence of fitness to lead. Meanwhile, Romsca's protection of Durral tests moral categories through a personal relationship. Together these strands ask what a community should preserve, what it should relinquish and whom it can recognize as worthy of care. [PL-P01; PL-P04; PL-P07–08]

Ublaz's island is an instructive economic setting because its political power has a material basis: no local trees grow there, and he controls timber needed for ship repairs. Access depends on tribute. His hypnotic authority and armed agents matter, but so does the ability to keep other people dependent on a supply. This is a sharper design reference than “pirate king wants wealth.” It does not supply timber prices, replenishment rates or a sustainable trade simulation. [PL-P01]

Grath's recovery is equally material. Helpers provide shelter and food before she can resume action. She seals her family's home as a tomb and makes recognizable arrows. The weapon carries both workmanship and memory. Her later certainty about enemies is understandable in that history, but the novel also contains Romsca, whose conduct complicates such a blanket claim. [PL-P02; PL-P04–05]

## Care does not require a clean prior identity

Romsca is a corsair, and Durral's gratitude does not depend on pretending she was always an Abbey resident. The inspected death scene proceeds through practical attention: shelter, warmth, food, water and attempted comfort. Her own competence persists as she advises him about the ship's course and possible contacts. She receives care while still contributing knowledge. This is stronger than a token “good ferret” exception because the relationship develops through acts and changing forms of address. [PL-P04]

The scene is not evidence for universal redemption or safe recruitment. It also does not establish that mercy works only when its beneficiary dies. Those would both be inappropriate gameplay rules extracted from one plot. Under DEC-005, the useful lesson is to keep identity, observed action, need and membership decisions distinct. Durral's later memory preserves the relationship without making Romsca an invisible numerical adjustment to his leadership. [PL-P04; PL-P08]

Grath's conversation with Inbar prevents archery skill from becoming a synonym for combat experience. He can shoot well without having aimed at a living target. Her poem preserves history but also renews a commitment to vengeance. The same art form can mourn, warn and mobilize; “community song” is not automatically a cheerful morale device. Her later decision to live elsewhere must not be rewritten as compulsory permanent Abbey membership. [PL-P05–06]

## The treasure ending is not a simple economic rule

Tansy asks about returning the pearls to Grath and credits the other searchers. Durral attributes ownership to her with reference to Martin's message. She then rejects possession because of the lives associated with the treasure. That sequence is ethically meaningful but also contestable: the decision involves someone else's family property and the authority to determine its fate. Record the question rather than recasting the scene as a universally sufficient restitution process. [PL-P07]

The story rewards relinquishment with recognition as Abbess, but the game should not turn disposal into a profitable leadership exploit. The thematic point is an ordering of values, not a conversion recipe from jewels to authority. Equally, *Marlfox* values recovering a treasured tapestry, while *The Long Patrol* reuses treasure for memorial medals. The proper comparison asks what the object means, whose claim it carries and what retaining it does. It does not classify all precious objects as corrupt. [PL-P07; MX-P05; LP-P08]

The closing domestic arrangements keep leadership from becoming a total identity. Durral is happy to cook; Tansy continues learning with advice. The harvest is secured before festivities, and existing rooms can be adapted for elders' comfort. These are concrete ways to depict a community's changing needs without assuming an entirely new building for every function. [PL-P08]
