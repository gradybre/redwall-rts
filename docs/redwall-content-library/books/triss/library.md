# Triss — systematic content library

## Coverage and authority

All 3,857 normalized blocks were read sequentially across 19 untruncated chunks, including front matter and the final contents list. The story has three parts and 42 numbered chapters. `coverage.json` records every inspected interval and exact source unit. Every record supplies EPUB-member and unit-block locators in `catalog.json`; the Markdown index uses global block numbers. This is a full-reading, systematic content extraction, not a claim that no future editor could discover another useful minor reference.

Source assertions, character testimony, beliefs, uncertain observations and AI game completions remain separate. The six closing diarists are Malbun, Churk, Merola, Scarum, Sleeve and Apodemus. Their accounts are not interchangeable with an omniscient narrator. Promotional blurbs and author biography are not additions to the Triss-era cast.

## Contradictions and identity decisions

| Issue | Preserved resolution |
|---|---|
| Three-headed monster | Three independently named sapient adders entangled by a mace chain; crown stones produce extra-eye impressions. No new three-headed species. |
| Mokug as sole survivor | His fuller account names eight survivors including Agarnu and six Ratguards, 2519. Earlier sole-survivor wording is limited knowledge. |
| Royal literacy | RHOR script is a restricted learned code; Sleeve and Scummy provide clear examples of literacy outside royal status. |
| Three different boats | Stopdog is Kroova's old ketch and burns. New royal craft becomes Shogg. Seascab becomes Freedom. The proposed Scofftub name is rejected. |
| Welfo in Shogg's last vision | Welfo is alive on Peace Island and appears later; dying perception is not evidence of her death or teleportation. |
| Bluddbeak's fate | Mourned after failing to return, without a recovered body shown; Ovus's death is directly established. |
| Kurda's final encounter | Triss humiliates and disarms her; Kurda dies falling on her own broken blade. The companions' mercy ideal does not erase the conduct contradiction. |
| Source heroes and cruelty | Net-bound foes are beaten, the Abbot imposes a lettuce-and-water punishment, and liberation troops take no prisoners. These are source facts, explicitly rejected as player policies. |
| Agarnu's death | Former bearers float his litter into the fjord while Sagax restrains Triss. Do not invent a lawful trial, consent or a rescue motive not stated. |
| Bloodwrath | Narrator describes invulnerability, but Sagax later has incomplete recall. The project uses rare consequential loss of control, not literal invulnerability. |
| Brockhall rear cap | Turn halfway and slide south; left-to-right viewing direction is not enough for a surveyed clockwise bearing. |
| Camp continuity | The moved fir camp and later discovery of Slitfang beside the axe stuck during the prior quarrel may compress locations; do not force a precise unsupported layout. |
| Botanical words | Splurge/spurge laurel and narrative moss/liverwort remain visible as source wording distinctions. No real medical or foraging reliability is asserted. |
| Dormitory punishment and child threats | Tail-cutting, boiling jokes, food deprivation and locked labor are excluded; game consequences need fair restitution and continued care. |
| Royal objects given to infant | Symbolic rejection of dynastic authority, not a real infant-safety model or a requirement to destroy all historical artifacts. |

## Recipe rules

The recipe catalog includes served foods, proposed menus, dream foods, song references, rejected joke mixtures and ambiguous diary-stain identifications. Occurrence status is explicit in each record. Cream, cheese, shrimp, rudd, sprats, woodpigeon and eggs retain their source names, while separate proposed game versions use oat cream, cultured hazelnut preparations, mussels, dace, herring, mushrooms or chickpea dumplings. Generic October Ale, Streambank Stew and other incomplete foods receive labeled AI base ingredients, not fabricated canonical recipes. All ingredients and instructions remain game concepts; source remedies and uncertain plants establish neither clinical efficacy nor safe real-world consumption.
